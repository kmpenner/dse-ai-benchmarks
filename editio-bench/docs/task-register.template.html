Mechanics

How a run works

One command — editio-bench phase — walks every (task × model) pair, collects an answer, then hands that answer to a two-model panel for grading against the task's own ground truth, resolving a panel split with a third scorer.

Step one

Capability check

OpenRouter's model catalogue is queried up front. A model whose input_modalities lack image is skipped on the three image-bearing tasks and recorded as skipped, never scored.

Step two

Answer

The philologist system prompt, the task prompt, and — where the task has one — the manuscript crop as a base64 image_url part. Temperature 0, reasoning effort set by editio-bench phase --reasoning {none,low,high} (default low) — a whole run can be repeated at a different effort to compare cost/latency/quality trade-offs. Wall-clock latency and cost are both recorded per task; a cache hit replays the original run's real figures rather than reporting zero.

Step three

Score

Each answer goes to both panel scorers with the rubric as system prompt and the task prompt, ground truth, and candidate answer as the user turn. If they disagree by more than 10 points, a third scorer is called and the final score is the median of all three rather than the raw two-scorer mean.

Step four

Record

One CSV row per (task × model): answer text, per-scorer score, score_consensus, whether a tiebreak fired, cost, latency, and status. Phase means and an ALL_PHASES mean are computed from score_consensus.

The register

Tasks, by witness

Tasks are grouped by the manuscript they come from rather than by type, because the ground truth of each is bound to a specific folio and its specific scribal habits. Only the transcription tasks send an image; every other task carries all its evidence in the prompt text, so a hallucinated reading is always a fabrication rather than a misreading.

Grading

Scoring rubrics

Six rubrics, one per task type, each serialized into the scorer's system prompt. They share a common conviction: a confident invention scores far worse than an acknowledged gap.

Known limits

Transcription ground truth comes from published editions

The Greek, Latin, and Aramaic transcriptions are checked against Puech, Chaparro Gómez, and the corrected TEI files. A model that has memorized those editions can recite a near-perfect answer without reading the plate at all — and until the image-path defect was fixed, some models did exactly that, scoring 100 on tasks where no image had been attached.

Transcription scores recorded before that fix, and any score for a model without vision support, measure recall rather than reading. The suite now refuses to score a vision-less model on an image task; the historical contaminated rows have been retired to archive/superseded_16task_master_suite/ rather than left in the live comparison. The benchmark still has no memorization control — an unpublished or deliberately altered fragment — to separate the two abilities.

The two scorers do not agree

On the same answers, minimax-m3 grades consistently harsher than gemini-3.8-flash — by roughly 25 points on transcription, 26 on encoding, and 12 on collation. A third scorer now resolves any single task where the gap exceeds 10 points (see Mechanics above), but that only settles individual disagreements as they occur — it doesn't change the panel's overall lean. Treat a single-scorer figure as one opinion, and read the spread as a property of the panel rather than noise.

Restraint is hard to grade

Three rubrics explicitly award credit for declining to read illegible ink, and the Aramaic ground truth says so outright. In practice a model that refuses to commit to a doubtful letter can still score below one that guesses well, because the refusal leaves the scorer with nothing to match. This is the suite's least settled scoring behaviour.
